Haneda Departure Day: Choose a Last Tokyo Stop

Tokyo Station, Shinagawa and a compact Tokyo district can be practical before Haneda. Compare the entire route, not the distance on a map.

Quick answer

Start near the Keikyu corridor in Shinagawa for a station meal, or stay near Tokyo Station for a facade-and-souvenir stop. Longer shrine, market or bay routes are useful only when their visiting hours fit as well as the flight buffer.

Make the airport target first

This planner uses a 3-hour airport-ready target before the flight as its conservative default. It is a planning policy, not a universal airline rule. Subtract luggage pickup, the journey and station navigation before spending the remaining time. Follow any earlier target from your airline.

Choose one final experience

Akihabara

Choose one anime, electronics or game store and one floor to browse; skip release-day queues.

Asakusa & Sensoji

Walk from Kaminarimon toward the main temple grounds, then turn back without adding a river cruise.

Ginza

Choose one department store or wagashi shop on a single block instead of crossing the whole district.

Ginza Evening

Choose a booked dinner or a restaurant with immediate seating, followed by one short street walk.

Hamamatsucho & Zojoji

Choose Zojoji’s exterior and temple approach, then return toward the station without adding a deck ticket.

Haneda Airport

Resolve terminal and counter questions first, then use one nearby meal or shop if airline procedures permit.

Harajuku

Choose one short shopping street pass and a single store; turn around if crowd movement becomes slow.

Kanda & Jimbocho

Choose two bookshops on one street and a nearby coffee stop; avoid searching every specialist shelf.

Kappabashi

Choose one product category and two adjacent kitchenware shops; allow time for packing before leaving.

Meiji Shrine

Follow the south approach to the main sanctuary and return the same way; skip the garden and museum on a short visit.

Nihonbashi

See the bridge and one traditional food or craft stop on a compact one-way walk.

Odaiba

Choose one waterfront viewpoint near your arrival point, then return without adding a second mall.

Omotesando

Walk one avenue segment and choose one design shop or café instead of a full fashion-shopping itinerary.

Roppongi & Azabudai

Choose one public space, café or architectural view; skip ticketed observation queues when time is tight.

Shibuya

Choose the crossing and one nearby seated break; book a viewpoint only if its slot ends well before departure.

Shinagawa

Use one station-side meal and souvenir stop rather than leaving for another Tokyo district.

Shiodome

Choose one plaza or pedestrian-deck loop and a coffee break, keeping the return point in sight.

Tokyo Station & Marunouchi

See the Marunouchi red-brick facade, then choose one station souvenir zone before returning to your airport route.

Tokyo Tower

Choose the exterior view with Zojoji or a reserved deck visit; use the exterior if the ticket queue is uncertain.

Toyosu

Choose one breakfast venue and a short nearby bay walk; skip auction viewing unless its separate requirements are met.

Tsukiji Outer Market

Choose one food priority and a short outer-market browse; skip a famous stall when the queue grows.

Ueno & Ameyoko

Choose one Ameyoko pass and a no-queue meal; a museum needs its own admission and closing-time check.

When to go directly to the airport

Skip another stop if the live route threatens your planning target, luggage collection is uncertain, or the facility will close before your visit ends. The airport itself is an option only after terminal and airline questions are resolved.
