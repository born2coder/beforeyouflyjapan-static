Is Haneda Airport Open 24 Hours? Overnight & Early Flights

Haneda Terminal 3 is generally open 24 hours, but that rule does not apply to every terminal or service. The airport says T3 overnight stays are for people who have used or will use international flights. A domestic-only early departure is not a reason to assume T3 is available as accommodation.

Area or serviceWhat to plan around
T3 buildingGenerally 24 hours, with international-flight eligibility for overnight stays under airport rules.
T1 / T2 domestic areasGenerally 05:00–24:00, with lobby operation tied to last flights. Overnight lobby stays after closure are generally unavailable.
T2 international departuresOpens at 05:00; the departure lobby remains open until after the last departure. Do not borrow T3’s 24-hour rule.
Airline counter / bag drop / securityOperation follows the relevant airline, flight and checkpoint. The building’s opening time is not your counter’s opening time.
Food, shops and transportEach has its own schedule. A 24-hour terminal does not imply all-night restaurants or continuous trains.

Choose between an eligible wait and a bookable bed

An eligible public-side overnight wait in T3 provides no reserved bed, quiet space or guaranteed charging seat. Keep flight details and documents accessible and follow staff directions. For travelers with children, a long flight ahead or a need for reliable sleep, a bookable room can be a better use of the night.

The airport hotel directory lists The Royal Park Hotel Tokyo Haneda at T3, Haneda Excel Hotel Tokyu at T2 and First Cabin Haneda Terminal 1. Choose using the actual route to your departure terminal at the time you need it. Do not assume an “airport hotel” has a shuttle running early enough, and do not confuse public-side hotel rooms with transit-only facilities. See the airport’s hotel locations and provider links.

A 00:30 flight is on the ticket’s next date

Flight: 21 November, 00:30. Three-hour terminal-ready target: 20 November, 21:30. Travel on the evening of 20 November. Arriving on the evening of 21 November would be a day late for this flight.

If the complete journey takes 90 minutes and bag collection 20 minutes in your selected example, begin collection at 19:40 on 20 November. Enter the ticket’s flight date in the planner and the previous date for your free time. It uses Japan Standard Time (JST).

For a 06:30 flight, the first train may not solve the journey

Under this site’s three-hour policy, a 06:30 departure gives a 03:30 target. Compare the airline’s actual instructions with transport operating hours before choosing where to spend the night. If a suitable train does not run, the options are an eligible T3 overnight wait, accommodation with a workable access plan, or a prearranged road transfer.

Keikyu publishes late-night/early-morning access guidance from its airport-access page. Use an actual dated route for the chosen terminal. A road transfer also needs a real pickup location, availability and a budget; it should not be an unplanned assumption after missing the last train.

Make the overnight wait easier before leaving the city

  • Eat before travel if your preferred terminal restaurant will be closed. Keep a drink or simple food plan that respects facility rules.
  • Keep baby supplies, medication, documents and a charger outside the case until bag drop really opens.
  • Use storage only if you can reach and retrieve from that exact service during its operating hours.
  • For a short break, T3’s deck is listed as open 24 hours, subject to weather closure. It is not a sleeping facility.

If your airline asks you to arrive earlier: use that earlier terminal-ready time. The current planner uses a fixed three-hour international allowance and has no field for an earlier airline target. Do not accept a suggested route unless it also fits the earlier requirement; shorten it or go directly to the airport.
