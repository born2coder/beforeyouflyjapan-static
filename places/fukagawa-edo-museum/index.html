ONE LAST JAPAN STOP

Fukagawa Edo Museum with children before a flight

Fukagawa Edo Museum works best as one indoor visit: descend into a reconstructed Edo neighborhood, explore a few rooms and leave before the exhibits become tiring. It is a smaller, more manageable choice than trying to combine several museums before a flight.

Look for a home, a shop and a changing sky

The main exhibition recreates Fukagawa Saga-cho in the late Edo period at full scale. Start by looking across the street scene, then choose a dwelling and a shopfront to explore. Notice how lighting and sound suggest different times of day; everyday tools make a useful conversation starter with children.

Budget about 45–60 minutes in the exhibition and another 15–20 minutes for tickets, toilets and a pause. Follow the current signs about entering rooms and handling objects. The museum is the activity here; Kiyosumi Garden and a café crawl are optional separate visits, not free additions to the same allowance.

Use the museum entrance in Shirakawa

Set the destination to Fukagawa Edo Museum, 1-3-28 Shirakawa, Koto City, near Kiyosumi-shirakawa Station. Use the museum’s access and barrier-free diagrams for the station approach and the lift to exhibition level. A route via stairs may not suit a stroller or a large case.

On arrival, deal with tickets and the toilet before starting the reconstructed street. For a child who wants to spend longer inside a room, cut another room rather than making the return journey faster. The flight-aware plan includes a separate family/rest allowance; the local estimate here does not replace it.

Tickets and the closing-day trap

The reviewed tariff is ¥400 for adults and ¥50 for elementary, junior-high and high-school students or equivalent. Exhibition hours are 09:30–17:00, last admission 16:30. The second and fourth Mondays are normally closed except national holidays, with year-end and temporary closures also listed by the museum.

This is a good rain option only on a day it actually opens. If closed, do not substitute an unfamiliar distant museum at the last minute. Keep to a nearby open café or head toward your airport route. English-speaking volunteer assistance may be available, but an English tour is not included or guaranteed.

Leave bags where you can collect them on the way back

Find Kiyosumi-shirakawa Station on the map ↗

The museum’s operator says its history and cultural facilities, including Fukagawa Edo Museum, have free lockers. For a visit here, that is a more direct option than dropping a bag at Tokyo Station first. Locker dimensions and an oversized-bag service are not established in the FAQ: keep large suitcases at your hotel or another confirmed counter if needed, and collect your belongings before leaving the museum.
