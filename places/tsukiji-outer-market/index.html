Tsukiji Outer Market: Hours, Map & a Short Food Walk

Taste the everyday food culture behind Japanese cooking through seafood, tamagoyaki, tea, knives and small specialist stalls in a lively working market district.

YOUR VISIT, EXPLAINED

Tsukiji Outer Market is the food-shopping district in Tsukiji, not the wholesale market at Toyosu. Visit for small food shops and a meal. There is no single opening time for the whole district; choose an open shop and a date from the market calendar.

A 60–90 minute food visit, not a queue marathon

Start with one thing you actually want to eat: a seafood bowl, tamagoyaki or a small snack. Give the first part of the visit to that choice, then browse one lane for tea, pantry ingredients or kitchen goods. Keep the final 10–15 minutes for finding your return route. This is time in the market only; the airport journey and luggage are extra.

If a queue threatens your leave-by time, choose another open shop. Eating one good meal is more useful than spending the whole stop waiting for a famous counter. Follow each shop’s rules for where to eat and return rubbish to the appropriate collection point.

Find the outer market and keep the return simple

Use Tsukiji Station on the Hibiya Line or Tsukijishijo on the Oedo Line as your route anchor. The market’s official access map places the food streets and information centre; choose the station that matches your onward journey rather than simply the closest entrance.

Where to ask about bags and an open shop

Plat Tsukiji provides maps, English-language assistance and coin lockers, including larger sizes. Its English listing gives information-counter hours of 08:00–14:00 on weekdays/Saturdays and 10:00–14:00 on Sundays, holidays and market closure days, with year-end closures. Special notices can change these hours. Do not assume locker access lasts all evening, and do not count on an empty compartment.

If there is no suitable storage, choose a meal near your departure station or collect your hotel bags and go to the airport. Narrow shopping lanes are a poor place to spend your final hour managing a suitcase.

YOUR LAST STOP, IN PRACTICE

One visit worth making

Choose one food priority and one short browsing lane in Tsukiji Outer Market. If the famous stall has a long queue, buy from an open alternative or eat at the airport.

What to budget

Street browsing is free; food prices and shop hours vary. Use the market calendar before choosing a date.

Find the entrance and keep bags on the return route

Route anchor: Tsukiji or Tsukijishijo Station.

Use the outer-market street map for your chosen stall and return station. This is not Toyosu Market and does not include a trip to the tuna auction.

Open Tsukiji or Tsukijishijo Station on the map ↗

Keep collection on the Tsukiji return route. Before walking to Tsukiji or Tsukijishijo Station, confirm a locker or staffed counter, its closing time and the exact gate side. Local storage prices have not been verified for this stop; use the posted rate rather than assuming the same price as another station. If no suitable space is available, keep the bag and choose a station meal or the airport instead.

WHY GO

Why this place is worth your time

A dense food district of seafood, produce, knives, pantry goods and small places to eat.

What to notice: Choose one or two things to eat well instead of queuing for everything, and watch how specialist shops focus on a single craft or ingredient.

Highlights

  • Fresh seafood and grilled snacks
  • Tamagoyaki and pantry specialties
  • Professional food and knife shops
