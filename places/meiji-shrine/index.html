ONE LAST JAPAN STOP

Meiji Jingu before a Haneda flight

Meiji Jingu is a woodland walk to an active shrine, not an attraction immediately outside Harajuku’s gate. Use the south entrance, reach the main sanctuary and return the same way; the approach itself deserves time.

South entrance → sanctuary → the same entrance

From the Harajuku-side south entrance, follow the principal approach through the torii and wooded grounds toward the main shrine buildings. Pause at the barrel displays on the way and leave the main sanctuary area respectfully after your visit. Retracing the route keeps the exit familiar.

The shrine says the entrance-to-main-buildings walk takes at least another ten minutes after reaching an entrance. Our suggested 50–70 minute local visit includes walking both ways and unhurried pauses. It does not include Meiji Jingu Museum, the Inner Garden or a separate Harajuku shopping trip. Those facilities would need their own hours, admission and walking time.

A station arrival is only the start of the approach

Use Harajuku Station or Meiji-jingumae for the south approach and the shrine’s official access map to locate the entrance. Do not use a different gate on the return simply because a map shows another nearby station; that changes the walking distance and onward route.

Much of this stop is outdoors. With a stroller, mobility aid or tired child, allow extra time for the surface and choose the official accessible route rather than assuming every path is equivalent. The shrine lists wheelchairs at the entrances, but availability and your individual access needs still matter.

Winter closing comes early

The grounds are free to enter and open daily, but hours change by month with daylight. The reviewed official table gives November 06:10–16:10 and December 06:40–16:00; summer closing is later. Use the complete monthly table for another season rather than carrying a winter or summer time into the wrong month.

Plan to finish the sanctuary visit early enough to walk back before closing. If rain, fatigue or the time remaining makes that unpleasant, choose a seated break on the Harajuku side instead. A late-afternoon woodland walk cannot simply be shifted into the evening after the shrine closes. Museum and garden opening times may be shorter than grounds hours.
