WHY GO

Why this place is worth your time

A Shinto shrine and forest beside Harajuku, approached along a broad gravel path.

What to notice: Notice how quickly the sound of Harajuku fades after the first torii, and pause before each gate rather than rushing straight to the main hall.

Highlights

  • The huge wooden torii gates
  • The shift from city noise to forest quiet
  • The broad approach to the main sanctuary

DEPARTURE-DAY FIT

How to use Meiji Shrine before your flight

Meiji Shrine is strongest as an early, reflective stop before shops open in western Tokyo. The wooded approach feels removed from the city, yet the long gravel paths mean the visit takes more walking than the station-to-gate distance suggests.

Gate hours change with the season, and individual facilities may follow different schedules. For departure day, treat the main approach and shrine area as the complete visit. A garden, museum or Harajuku shopping extension should only be added when the planner provides a separate margin.

A practical one-way visit

  1. Use the entrance closest to your transport plan and begin with a firm turnaround time.
  2. Walk the main approach to the shrine, pause respectfully, and return by the same route.
  3. Collect luggage and continue toward Haneda without adding a retail detour.

When to skip it

Skip this visit in severe weather, when long walking is difficult, or when a Narita transfer would require crossing the city after the shrine.

Before you commit

  • Check the official seasonal opening times for the date.
  • Wear shoes suitable for a long gravel approach.
  • Keep food, photography and behaviour consistent with posted shrine guidance.
