Tokyo Tower: Location, Tickets & Time for a Short Visit

Take in Tokyo from an enduring postwar landmark whose red-and-white silhouette remains one of the city’s most recognizable symbols.

YOUR VISIT, EXPLAINED

Tokyo Tower is in Shibakoen, Minato City, not Ginza. An exterior photo stop and a trip up to the observation deck are different visits. Choose which matters to you before committing to a queue.

Choose the view you have time for

VisitCost and time to plan
Exterior from public streetsNo observation ticket needed. Allow roughly 20–30 minutes locally for views and photos; walking from the station is extra.
Main Deck, 150 mAdult ¥1,500; high-school ¥1,200; elementary/junior-high ¥900; age 4+ ¥600. Allow roughly 60–90 minutes for the visit, with additional time if queues are long.
Top Deck Tour, 150 m + 250 mAdult ¥3,300 online or ¥3,500 at the counter on the reviewed tariff. It includes the Main Deck. Choose only when the booked slot and the full visit fit your airport deadline.

These visit lengths are editorial allowances, not operator-guaranteed durations. A timed ticket does not make the airport journey shorter.

Opening hours and when to skip the ascent

The Main Deck is listed as 09:00–23:00, with last admission at 22:30; crowding can bring admission forward. The Top Deck Tour is listed as 09:00–22:45, last tour 22:15, and can be suspended for weather or operational reasons.

If visibility is poor or the queue uses up the spare time, enjoy the tower from outside and keep the journey comfortable. Do not add a second paid attraction simply because you have already reached the building.

Use Shibakoen as the location, not Ginza

The address is 4-2-8 Shibakoen, Minato City. Compare the official approaches from Akabanebashi, Onarimon, Kamiyacho and the Hamamatsucho corridor. The nearest station for arrival may not be the best one for your airport return. Walking, crossings and station entrances belong in the journey time.

Keep a short stop short

Store large luggage on the airport-return route before climbing or joining a queue. If using a stroller or travelling with someone who needs lifts, use the operator’s accessibility guidance and allow longer for the station approach. A photo outside followed by a relaxed meal can be a better final memory than rushing down from the deck.

YOUR LAST STOP, IN PRACTICE

One visit worth making

Choose either the Tokyo Tower exterior with Zojoji or a reserved observation-deck visit. If ticket queues are uncertain, enjoy the exterior view and return on schedule.

What to budget

Exterior viewing is free; observation decks require tickets and have different products and prices.

Find the entrance and keep bags on the return route

Route anchor: Onarimon, Akabanebashi or Hamamatsucho.

Choose the station used by your return airport route. The tower is not directly on Hamamatsucho's monorail platform; retain the walking allowance.

Open Onarimon, Akabanebashi or Hamamatsucho on the map ↗

Keep collection on the Hamamatsucho return route. Before walking to Onarimon, Akabanebashi or Hamamatsucho, confirm a locker or staffed counter, its closing time and the exact gate side. Local storage prices have not been verified for this stop; use the posted rate rather than assuming the same price as another station. If no suitable space is available, keep the bag and choose a station meal or the airport instead.

WHY GO

Why this place is worth your time

A 333-meter broadcasting tower and observation landmark near Zojoji.

What to notice: Before going up, step back and see the full structure from ground level; the tower’s relationship with the surrounding city is part of its appeal.
