WHY GO

Why this place is worth your time

Tokyo’s principal intercity station and the business district facing its restored Marunouchi building.

What to notice: View the facade from the plaza before going inside, then look for regional food and gifts that bring the rest of Japan into one station.

Highlights

  • The Marunouchi red-brick facade
  • Station bento and regional souvenirs
  • The old-station and modern-skyline contrast

DEPARTURE-DAY FIT

How to use Tokyo Station & Marunouchi before your flight

Tokyo Station and Marunouchi are practical for both airport directions because the area is already a major departure hub. The red-brick station frontage, underground shopping and department stores can form a strong final stop without committing to another district.

The station is enormous. Choose the Marunouchi side, Yaesu side or one underground zone, and do not switch sides casually. If you are using an airport bus or reserved train, locate the exact boarding point and obtain the ticket before browsing.

A practical one-way visit

  1. Store luggage and physically locate the platform, ticket office or bus stop for the airport leg.
  2. Visit one station side for the façade, a meal or a final purchase.
  3. Return to the boarding point with time for vertical movement and gate checks.

When to skip it

Skip underground exploration if you are already disoriented, if luggage storage is full, or if your airport service requires a fixed reservation you have not secured.

Before you commit

  • Confirm whether the route leaves from Tokyo Station, a nearby bus terminal or another named stop.
  • Add at least one station-navigation allowance beyond train time.
  • Keep food queues and souvenir checkout outside the boarding buffer.
