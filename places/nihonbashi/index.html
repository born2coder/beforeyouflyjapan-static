Nihonbashi: Meaning, Location & a Short Tokyo Walk

Walk through Tokyo’s merchant origins around the bridge that became the historic starting point of Japan’s major roads, now surrounded by enduring shops and department stores.

YOUR VISIT, EXPLAINED

Nihonbashi means “Japan Bridge.” It names both the historic bridge and the surrounding Tokyo district, northeast of Tokyo Station. If you want to see the bridge, use the bridge as your map destination rather than a department store or a broad neighborhood pin.

Why stop at this bridge?

Nihonbashi became the starting point of the five major roads of Edo. The bridge still marks the reference point used for distances from Tokyo. That makes it a small, tangible way to understand the old city: look at the bridge and river, then the commercial streets that grew around them.

It is a city bridge, not a large admission-based attraction. A short stop works best when you combine it with one nearby shop or a tea break, rather than expecting a long sightseeing complex.

A 45–60 minute bridge-and-shop visit

Start at Nihonbashi Bridge, allow 10–15 minutes to see it from the public pavement, then choose one nearby food shop or department-store food floor for browsing. Keep the last 10–15 minutes for returning to your chosen station. These are local visit allowances; a seated meal, a queue or a long Tokyo Station walk needs extra time.

For a rainy day, give more time to an open indoor shop and less to the bridge. Food floors are not always seating areas, so decide where you will eat before buying a full meal.

Nihonbashi, Mitsukoshimae or Tokyo Station?

Nihonbashi and Mitsukoshimae stations serve different parts of the district. Tokyo Station is another option, but leaving its platforms and crossing to the Yaesu side is part of the walk, not zero-time access. Choose the station that matches your arrival and airport return.

When this is a good last stop

Choose it when you want a compact city walk and can leave your luggage along the return route. With bags still at Tokyo Station, keep collection in the plan. If you already feel tired, one nearby café is enough; the bridge does not need an ambitious itinerary around it.

YOUR LAST STOP, IN PRACTICE

One visit worth making

See Nihonbashi Bridge, then choose one food or craft stop in the COREDO Muromachi area. Keep the return route on the same side of the district instead of adding Tokyo Station shopping.

What to budget

The bridge walk is free; food, shopping and exhibitions are extra.

Find the entrance and keep bags on the return route

Route anchor: Nihonbashi or Mitsukoshimae Station.

Choose which station serves your airport route before walking. Mitsukoshimae and Nihonbashi are not interchangeable entrances to the same platform.

Why this place is worth your time
