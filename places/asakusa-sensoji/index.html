Sensoji & Asakusa: Opening Hours and a Short Temple Walk

Come to Sensoji for the temple approach, or for the temple plus Nakamise shopping—but choose the time accordingly. The main hall opens much earlier than most shopping activity, and closes before an evening walk is over.

Kaminarimon to the main hall, then back

Begin at Kaminarimon, walk along Nakamise, pass through Hozomon and continue to the main hall. This gives the visit a clear end point instead of an ever-expanding list of side streets. On the return, buy a small souvenir or ningyo-yaki cake only if the line is short.

Allow around 60–90 minutes locally for this walk, pauses and limited browsing. Leave more room if crowds are moving slowly. A river cruise, kimono rental and Tokyo Skytree are separate activities; including one means building a different day rather than squeezing it into the same visit.

Return to the Asakusa station you actually need

Asakusa is served by Tokyo Metro, Toei, Tobu and Tsukuba Express, but they are not one shared set of platforms. Save the entrance for the operator used on your next journey before heading to Kaminarimon. In particular, a sign for Tsukuba Express does not take you to the Toei Asakusa Line.

The temple approach can be crowded and is not a convenient place to wheel a large case. With a stroller or someone who tires easily, keep to this direct out-and-back route, allow longer for crossings and use the station’s accessible-exit map rather than the nearest staircase.

The hall, the grounds and the shops keep different hours

Sensoji gives main-hall hours of 06:00–17:00 from April to September and 06:30–17:00 from October to March. These are hall hours, not Nakamise’s opening hours. The ordinary temple approach does not require a general admission ticket; offerings, food and purchases are extra.

Early morning suits a temple-focused visit without promising open shops. After 17:00, treat it as an exterior and neighborhood walk, not entry to the main hall. If rain or congestion makes the approach uncomfortable, shorten the browsing rather than missing the return station.
