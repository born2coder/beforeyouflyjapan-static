Sensoji & Asakusa: Opening Hours and a Short Temple Walk

Follow the classic approach from Kaminarimon through Nakamise to Sensoji, experiencing temple ritual, traditional shopping and old-Tokyo street life in one clear sequence.

YOUR VISIT, EXPLAINED

Asakusa has no single opening time. Sensoji’s main hall opens 06:00–17:00 from April to September and 06:30–17:00 from October to March. Nakamise shops and other temple facilities keep separate hours.

Your visitWhat to expect
Early morningA temple-focused visit can work before the shops open. Do not promise yourself breakfast or souvenir shopping along a fully open Nakamise.
DaytimeBest for combining the main hall with shops that are open. Leave room for crowds at Kaminarimon and the approach.
After 17:00The main hall is closed. Treat this as an exterior and neighborhood visit, with shop-by-shop availability; it is not the same temple visit as daytime.

Main-hall hours are from Sensoji itself. Special events, access restrictions and the hours of other halls can differ.

A simple route: Kaminarimon → Nakamise → the main hall

Allow about 60–90 minutes locally for a compact temple walk and a little browsing, rather than trying to cover all of Asakusa. Pause at Kaminarimon, walk along Nakamise without blocking shop fronts, then continue through Hozomon toward the main hall. Keep a shorter version if the crowd is slow. The existing flight-aware planner may allocate a longer visit; use its complete route for your departure decision.

For an early visit, put the temple first and have breakfast elsewhere if shops are closed. For a shopping visit, avoid using the hall’s early opening time as evidence that Nakamise will be ready for you.

Asakusa Station can mean a different entrance

Ginza Line, Toei Asakusa Line, Tobu and Tsukuba Express services do not all use the same platforms or exits. Choose the station for your onward airport train and save its location before walking. A map pin simply labelled “Asakusa” is not a complete return route.

Large bags and crowded approaches make this less comfortable. Leave luggage on your return corridor and include collection time. With a stroller or limited mobility, keep the route to the main approach and use accessible station exits.

When the visit no longer fits

If the main hall has closed and that was your reason for coming, choose an evening neighborhood walk only if you still want it. If time is tight, skip extra side streets and head back to the correct station. Ueno and Ameyoko is an alternative area, not an automatic extra stop to add on top.

YOUR LAST STOP, IN PRACTICE

One visit worth making

Walk Kaminarimon → Nakamise → Sensoji, then retrace the approach. Buy one ningyo-yaki snack only if the queue fits; skip the river cruise on this compact route.

What to budget

The temple approach is free; food and purchases are extra.
