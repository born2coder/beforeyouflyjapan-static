YOUR LAST STOP, IN PRACTICE

One visit worth making

Use Ginza Mitsukoshi for a single food-hall or souvenir stop near the 4-chome crossing. If you prefer stationery, choose Itoya instead; do not try to do both on a short departure-day visit.

What to budget

Street browsing is free; food, shopping and exhibitions have separate charges.

Find the entrance and keep bags on the return route

Route anchor: Ginza Station, Ginza 4-chome crossing.

Choose your return station first. Ginza Station is not Tokyo Station, and a department-store basement can add extra time to reach the street.

If your route uses Tokyo Metro Ginza, the operator's October 2026 listing has lockers priced ¥500 / ¥600 / ¥900 / ¥1,000 depending on the compartment. These are Metro lockers, not a price promise for JR or another operator. Keep collection on your chosen route and check the posted charging period and gate side.

WHY GO

Why this place is worth your time

Central Tokyo’s best-known district for department stores, specialist shops, dining and modern architecture.

What to notice: Step into a depachika food hall or long-established specialty shop; the care in wrapping, display and service is as memorable as the purchase.

Highlights

  • Department-store food halls
  • Japanese stationery and craft
  • Architectural storefronts and evening lights
