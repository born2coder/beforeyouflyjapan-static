ONE LAST JAPAN STOP

Nakano Broadway collector shopping before Haneda

Nakano Broadway suits a collector who can choose two specialist shops. It is less effective as an early-morning visit or an open-ended hunt for a rare item: many Mandarake shops begin at noon, and stock changes.

Choose the type of collection before the floor

Use Mandarake’s floor guide rather than walking every corridor. The reviewed guide places the main store on 3F and Henya on 4F; Henya specializes in vintage toys and corporate novelty items. A manga-first visitor can spend most of the time at the main store; a vintage-toy fan can start with Henya and add one relevant specialist shop.

Keep around 40–50 minutes for the chosen shops and the remaining local allowance for moving between them, payment and the return through Sunmall. Inspect condition labels and packaging, particularly with secondhand goods. Older product photographs on a shop page are examples, not a stock reservation.

North exit, Sunmall, then Broadway

Mandarake’s operator gives five minutes from JR Nakano’s North Exit. Go through the Nakano Sunmall shopping arcade toward Broadway, then use the building’s current floor directory for the chosen shop. The arcade helps with rain, although not every stage of the journey is sheltered.

Return through the same arcade to Nakano Station. The linked flight plan may include an outward trip from Shinjuku; the 60–80 minute estimate here is the local visit only. Reaching Shinjuku and any stored bags afterward is additional journey time, not another piece of shopping time.

Noon opening and a clear spending limit

Mandarake lists 12:00–20:00 for its Nakano operation. Other Broadway shops have their own schedules. Ordinary browsing does not require a general admission ticket, while prices vary widely by rarity and condition. Decide a spending limit before falling into a comparison between several versions of the same item.

If staff need time to find an item or a payment line grows, keep the search to the time you genuinely have. Leave without buying rather than extending to another district. In crowded narrow aisles, a large suitcase makes the visit less pleasant for everyone; avoid planning this as a casual stop with all your luggage still in hand.

Leave bags where you can collect them on the way back

Find Nakano Station, north side on the map ↗

JR East lists coin lockers at Nakano Station. If you arrive with a suitcase, a suitable station locker can avoid taking it through Broadway’s narrower shop corridors. Store only after finding a compartment that fits, then collect on the same station route before your airport journey. The official facility listing does not establish prices, dimensions or the gate side of a specific bank; use the station map and the bank’s posted terms rather than assuming a large locker will be free.
